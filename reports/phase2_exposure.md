## 3. Risk Score Audit & Model Alignment

### Model Identification
In previous exports, `predictions.risk_score` was generated using a legacy continuous regression model:
- **Model**: `LGBMRegressor` point-prediction of next-day continuous $\text{PM}_{2.5}$
- **Transformation**: Gaussian tail exceedance probability:
  $$p = 1.0 - \Phi\left(\frac{90.0 - \hat{y}}{\sigma}\right) \quad (\sigma = 24.78\ \mu\text{g/m}^3)$$
- **Defect**: This regressor tail was applied across **all rows**, including days when ambient air was already severely degraded ($\text{PM}_{2.5} > 150\ \mu\text{g/m}^3$). This caused nearly 30% of all days in the dataset to register as "High Risk", completely misrepresenting the purpose of an acute spike early-warning system.

### Tier Distribution Comparison

Risk tiers are defined by the calibrated spike probability:
- **Nominal**: $p < 0.10$
- **Watch**: $0.10 \le p < 0.25$
- **Elevated**: $0.25 \le p < 0.50$
- **High**: $p \ge 0.50$

| Model & Evaluation Slice | Nominal (<10%) | Watch (10–25%) | Elevated (25–50%) | High (≥50%) | Notes |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Legacy LGBM Regressor (All 29,922 Rows)** | 50.3% | 11.8% | 8.1% | **29.8%** | Flawed: includes days already in Poor/Severe AQI. |
| **Legacy LGBM Regressor (Fresh Crossings Only, N=21,893)** | 68.7% | 16.1% | 10.2% | **5.0%** | Uncalibrated parametric tail overshoots high-risk days. |
| **Shipped Model: Calibrated LogReg (Phase 1 Shipped Test Split, N=26,998)** | **84.9%** | **8.1%** | **6.1%** | **1.0%** | **Exact match to shipped model benchmark** (`phase1_final_logreg.pkl`). |
| **Shipped Model: Calibrated LogReg (2019 Fresh-Crossing Test Rows, N=21,893)** | **78.5%** | **11.1%** | **9.0%** | **1.4%** | Shipped model evaluated on 2019 calendar test year. |

## 4. Population within 5 km of Monitoring Points (All 26 Cities)

### Population Raster Vintage & Technical Evaluation
- **Raster Used**: WorldPop High-Resolution Population Count for India (`ind_ppp_2017_1km_Aggregated_UNadj.tif`, 17.44 MB).
- **Baseline Year**: **2017** (UN-adjusted residential count).
- **100 m 2022 Raster Evaluation**:
  The project includes `ind_pop_2022_CN_100m_R2025A_v1.tif` (732 MB, 100 m resolution, $34,507 \times 35,040$ grid cells = 1.2 billion cells). We evaluated whether windowed reads could be run:
  1. The 2022 file uses LZW-compressed tiles (`Compression: 5`), which requires the Python `imagecodecs` package. `imagecodecs` is not installed in the environment.
  2. The GDAL/rasterio C-extension (`rasterio._warp`) is blocked by Windows Application Control policy.
  3. Loading the full array into memory via Pillow triggers a `DecompressionBombError` (requiring >4.8 GB uncompressed RAM).
  4. In contrast, the 2017 1 km UN-adjusted raster evaluates all 107 station buffers in 1.2 seconds using NumPy and Pillow with zero native binary dependencies.
  5. **Decision**: Retain the 2017 1 km UN-adjusted raster and document it transparently as the 2017 baseline year.

## 6. Assumptions & Limitations

1. **Station Radius as Ambient Exposure Proxy**:
   - Continuous station monitors serve as ambient background proxies for residential populations within 2 km and 5 km radii. Micro-scale gradients near major transportation arteries or industrial point sources cannot be resolved by regional monitors alone.
2. **Isotropic Geodesic Buffers**:
   - Geodesic buffers treat dispersion as radially symmetric. Real atmospheric transport is anisotropic, governed by local wind direction, velocity, and boundary layer venting.
3. **Night-Time Residential Census Allocation**:
   - WorldPop counts represent ambient residential night-time population disaggregated to grid cells. Daytime commuting into central business districts is not captured.
4. **Coordinate Precision Hierarchy**:
   - The 82 stations matched to CPCB station records reflect true sensor locations (`coord_quality = "station"`).
   - The 25 unmatched stations retain municipal reference coordinates (`coord_quality = "city_point"`). Population buffers for unmatched stations should be interpreted as general urban-center approximations.
   - Manual coordinates can be supplied via `scripts/coordinates_override.csv` without fabricating records (`coord_quality = "manual"`).

### 3. Exposure Audit: Chennai & Mumbai Deep Dive

#### Why Chennai 5 km Population Changed from 2.30M to 3.18M
In Phase 2c, Chennai had 4 stations:
- `TN001` (Alandur Bus Depot): `12.9975, 80.2006` (South Chennai)
- `TN004` (Velachery): `12.9815, 80.2180` (South Chennai, overlapping with Alandur)
- `TN002` & `TN003` (Manali): Matched to `13.1645, 80.2628` (North Chennai industrial belt near the coast).

Because coastal Manali lies in an industrial harbor district with relatively sparse residential census tracts, its 5 km buffer contained only ~800,000 residents. The union across Alandur, Velachery, and Manali summed to **2,295,958 (~2.30M)**.

In Phase 2d, following user instructions regarding duplicate-coordinate pairs, `TN002` and `TN003` were reverted from the northern industrial match to the **Chennai municipal reference point (`13.0878, 80.2785`)** with `coord_quality = "suspect"`. This point lies in George Town / Central Chennai, one of the densest residential urban cores in southern India (**1,540,723 residents within 5 km**). Because Central Chennai is 12 km north of Alandur/Velachery, its buffer has minimal spatial overlap with them. Taking the spatial union of Central Chennai (1.54M) and South Chennai (1.64M non-overlapping) increased the union footprint population to **3,180,164 (3.18M)**.

#### Why Mumbai Mean Daily Expected Exposed Went from 335K to 597K (Unchanged Coordinates)
This increase is driven entirely by a **window change effect combined with monitoring network expansion**:
1. **The Phase 2c Test Window (Jan 2019 – Dec 2019)**:
   - For the first 5 months of 2019 (January through May), **only 1 station was active in Mumbai** (`MH005` Bandra). On each day during those 5 months, daily expected exposure was computed over Bandra alone (which covers only 1.76M people in its buffer).
   - The remaining 9 Mumbai stations (`MH006` through `MH014`) came online only in June 2019.
   - Averaging 5 months of single-station data (low coverage) with 7 months of multi-station data produced an artificially depressed calendar-year daily average of **335,004**.
2. **The Phase 2d Test Window (Jul 2019 – Jun 2020)**:
   - Evaluates Mumbai strictly **after** all 10 stations were deployed. Across all 366 days in the Phase 1 final test window, all 10 stations were active, evaluating the full 8.87M-person multi-station union footprint every single day.
   - With an identical average calibrated risk score (~0.051), evaluating across the fully deployed 10-station network throughout the entire year yields the true mature-network daily expectation: **597,106**.

### 4. Shared Coordinate Audit & Twin Cities
Auditing all 107 reporting stations across 26 cities revealed exactly **one pair of cities sharing identical coordinates**:
- **Ernakulam** (`KL002`: Kacheripady, Kerala PCB) and **Kochi** (`KL004`: Vyttila, Kerala PCB) both share the coordinate `(9.9399, 76.2602)`.
- **Reason**: Ernakulam is the mainland urban administrative district of the Greater Kochi municipal agglomeration. Both stations currently fall in the unmatched set (`coord_quality = "city_point"`) and thus inherit the same municipal center reference point.
- **Flagged in JSON**: Both cities have been explicitly flagged in `alert_data.json` under `city_exposure` with `"is_shared_city_point": true` and `"shared_coordinates_with": ["Kochi"]` / `["Ernakulam"]`.
